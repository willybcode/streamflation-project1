## 3. Cross-check vs the independent Kaggle dataset

Kaggle's `streaming_service.csv` states it tracks the *lowest-cost, no-ads* plan per service per month.
We therefore compare it against the cheapest no-ads plan in our main dataset (discontinued plans allowed — they were the cheapest option while they existed).

| service | months compared | months matching | agreement % |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apple TV | 51 | 48 | 94.1 |
| Disney+ | 51 | 51 | 100.0 |
| HBO Max | 45 | 44 | 97.8 |
| Hulu | 100 | 95 | 95.0 |
| Netflix | 151 | 147 | 97.4 |
| Paramount+ | 35 | 35 | 100.0 |
| Peacock | 43 | 42 | 97.7 |
| Prime Video | 94 | 94 | 100.0 |

**Overall agreement: 97.5% of 570 overlapping service-months.**

Disagreements found: **14** service-months, in exactly two patterns (full table: `kaggle_mismatches.csv`):

1. **Recording lag (10 service-months — Hulu, Apple TV, HBO Max, Peacock at single change points).** The two datasets disagree only by *when* a change is dated: the tracker records exact effective dates (e.g. Hulu $14.99 from 2022-10-10) while Kaggle records the change one to three months later (Dec 2022). Same prices, different timestamps — the tracker's daily precision wins for event analysis, which is why Q2 uses its `effective_date`.
2. **Tier switch in Netflix (4 service-months, Oct 2023 – Jan 2024).** Kaggle jumps from $9.99 to **$15.49**, which is Netflix **Standard**'s price; the actual cheapest ad-free plan (Basic) went to **$11.99**. Kaggle spliced two tiers, producing a fictitious +55% increase. Our main dataset models tiers explicitly, so this cannot happen silently. Any +55% Netflix figure taken from the Kaggle file alone is a tier switch, not a price hike.

Only the Netflix pattern affects conclusions (it inflates a headline increase); the rest would only shift single months in a timeline.

## 4. Known caveats

- Prices are US standard monthly list prices; promotions, annual plans and bundles are excluded (scope of the source).
- `launch_date`/`launch_price` are the *first recorded* price change, which may post-date the plan's actual launch (e.g. Netflix's earliest recorded row is Nov 2010).
- Netflix Basic is flagged `discontinued`; the source notes it was phased out for new subscribers 2023-07 and ended for all subscribers 2024-07.
- CPI is national all-items inflation (BLS via FRED). It deflates prices; it does not claim anything about this industry's costs.
- **Missing values:** the CPI series has no published Oct 2025 value (BLS collection gap). It is filled by linear interpolation between Sep and Nov 2025 and flagged in `monthly_prices.csv` (`cpi_imputed`); the price data itself has no missing values.
